Digital Memory & Human–Technology Interaction

Research-led UX case study · Role: Research, Analysis, Design Synthesis

1. Background & Problem

Digital technologies increasingly mediate how people remember, retrieve, and interact with information. While these systems promise efficiency and convenience, they also introduce new risks: loss of agency, over-reliance on automated structures, and invisible power embedded in classification and algorithms.

The challenge: How can digital systems support human memory and decision-making without undermining understanding, autonomy, and trust?

2. Research Approach

This project combined theoretical research and critical analysis to examine how digital infrastructures shape human cognition and behaviour.

  • Analysing digital memory systems (e.g. archives, platforms, file structures)
  • Reviewing literature on human cognition, memory, and technology
  • Identifying how metadata, classification, and algorithms influence access and visibility of information

3. Key Insights

  • Digital systems often prioritise efficiency over understanding, leading users to rely on systems they do not fully comprehend.
  • Classification and metadata structures shape what is remembered and what is forgotten, embedding cultural and institutional bias.
  • Users rarely see or question these structures, which reduces transparency and limits meaningful engagement.

4. Design Implications

Based on these insights, I identified several design principles for human-centred digital systems:

  • Transparency: Make system logic and information structures more legible to users
  • Agency: Support users in understanding and controlling how information is stored and retrieved
  • Ethical responsibility: Design with awareness of cultural, social, and institutional impact

These principles could inform the design of digital archives, knowledge platforms, or AI-assisted information systems.

5. Reflection

This project strengthened my ability to translate complex research into actionable design insights. It reinforced my interest in human-centred, responsible design, particularly in contexts involving AI, public knowledge, and digital infrastructure.
